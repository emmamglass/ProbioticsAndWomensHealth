# Methods Paragraph: Pairwise Community Modeling

Pairwise community modeling was performed using the MICOM (Microbial Community Modeling) framework to simulate interactions between *Gardnerella vaginalis* and potential inhibitory partners. For each pairwise comparison, community models were constructed by combining genome-scale metabolic models (GSMMs) of *G. vaginalis* with either safe probiotic candidates (*Lactobacillus jensenii*) or BV-associated inhibitory strains (*Anaerococcus lactolyticus*, *Anaerococcus tetradius*, and *Fannyhessea vaginae*). Community simulations were performed using the cooperative tradeoff optimization algorithm (fraction = 0.5), which balances individual organism growth objectives with community-level fitness through quadratic programming optimization (solved using OSQP). Each pairwise interaction was simulated at three different initial relative abundance ratios (1:1, 1:10, and 10:1, representing *G. vaginalis*:partner) to assess how competitive dynamics vary with numerical dominance. From each simulation, we calculated growth suppression as the fractional reduction in *G. vaginalis* growth rate relative to its growth in monoculture, niche overlap as the Jaccard distance between the sets of nutrients imported by each organism (where lower values indicate greater resource competition), and D-lactate production flux as a potential mechanism of growth inhibition. These metrics were compared between safe candidates and BV-associated inhibitors to identify key metabolic features associated with effective *G. vaginalis* suppression and to inform engineering targets for probiotic strain development.
